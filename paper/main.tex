\begin{abstract}
EEG foundation models learn transferable representations from unlabeled recordings,
reducing reliance on costly task-specific annotations across clinical applications.
However, dominant self-supervised approaches either predict discrete codes produced
by a separately pretrained tokenizer, requiring a multi-stage training pipeline
(e.g., LaBraM), or reconstruct masked raw waveforms, devoting model capacity to
task-irrelevant noise (e.g., CBraMod). We ask whether either complexity is necessary
when many clinical EEG tasks are primarily determined by spectral structure. We
introduce \textbf{SpecBraM}, a codebook-free EEG foundation model trained with
\textbf{Masked Band-Power Prediction (MBP)}, which directly predicts the log
band-power of masked spatio-temporal patches using targets computed by a
\emph{fixed} filterbank. Using only $2{,}388$ hours of EEG---a random one-seventh
subset of the TUH EEG Corpus, comparable to the smallest prior foundation models and
roughly one-quarter of the data used by the representative foundation model
CBraMod---SpecBraM reaches state-of-the-art balanced accuracy on sleep staging
(ISRUC, HMC) and remains competitive across clinical spectral tasks. A full-data
control trained on seven times more EEG yields only marginal improvements,
underscoring that a pretraining objective aligned with EEG spectral structure
matters more than simply increasing data scale. SpecBraM is also highly
label-efficient: with as few as $1\%$ of downstream labels, a linear probe on its
frozen features already surpasses a randomly initialized encoder trained on
\emph{all} of them. We scope the method to spectral, clinical, and passive EEG, and
identify motor imagery as a principled limitation. Our code and pretrained weights are available in the supplementary materials.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Electroencephalography (EEG) is central to clinical neuroscience---from sleep
staging and seizure screening to the study of depression and cognitive workload---but
expert-labeled EEG is scarce and costly, which motivates self-supervised foundation
models that pretrain once on large unlabeled corpora and then transfer to many
downstream tasks with few labels. Large models pretrained on the TUH EEG Corpus
(TUEG) \cite{obeid2016tuh} have become the standard recipe for EEG representation
learning \cite{jiang2024labram,wang2025cbramod}.
The two dominant designs are both heavy. LaBraM \cite{jiang2024labram} first
trains a vector-quantized neural tokenizer and then predicts its discrete codes,
a two-stage pipeline whose first stage is itself a nontrivial spectral
autoencoder. CBraMod \cite{wang2025cbramod} reconstructs the raw masked signal
with a criss-cross transformer. Both inherit their pretext task from vision
(discrete tokens; masked pixels) rather than from the structure of EEG, as shown
in Fig.~\ref{fig:contrast}.

We start from a simple premise: the discriminative content of clinical EEG is
overwhelmingly \emph{spectral}. Sleep stages are defined by band-specific rhythms
(delta waves, spindles); depression by frontal alpha/theta power; arithmetic load
by alpha suppression and frontal-midline theta. If the signal that matters is
band power, the pretext task should predict band power directly---without a
codebook, and without reconstructing the raw waveform, which spends model capacity on
microvolt-level noise the downstream tasks never use.

We introduce \textbf{SpecBraM} (Spectral Brain Model), a codebook-free
spectral-prediction foundation model trained with \textbf{Masked Band-Power
Prediction (MBP)}. A learnable filterbank tokenizer decomposes each patch into
physiological bands before encoding; MBP then predicts masked patches in the
\emph{spectral} domain, regressing their log band-power against a \emph{fixed}
(non-learnable) filterbank target that cannot be collapsed to a trivial constant.
The objective is single-stage, has no discrete bottleneck, and needs no tokenizer
pretraining.

Beyond architectural simplicity, our central empirical finding concerns
\emph{data efficiency}. Prior EEG foundation models emphasize scale: CBraMod, for
instance, curates ${\sim}9{,}000$ hours of TUEG and reports that additional
pretraining data improves downstream accuracy. For spectral tasks we observe the
opposite. Pretrained on a random one-seventh of TUEG ($2{,}388$ hours,
comparable to the size of LaBraM's entire corpus), SpecBraM matches or surpasses
these methods on sleep staging, and a full-data control trained on the full
${\sim}17{,}666$ hours yields no measurable improvement (the two agree to within
$0.005$ balanced accuracy). We conclude that, for spectral clinical EEG, the pretext
objective---rather than the scale of the pretraining data---is the primary
determinant of downstream performance.

\noindent Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Masked Band-Power Prediction (MBP)}, a codebook-free spectral
objective: predict the log band-power of masked EEG patches against a fixed
filterbank target. Single-stage, no vector quantization, no reconstruction of raw
noise. A fixed target is necessary---a learnable target collapses.
\item \textbf{Data efficiency}: with $2{,}388$ pretraining hours (${\sim}1/4$
of CBraMod, ${\sim}1/7$ of the full TUEG) the model reaches state-of-the-art
balanced accuracy on sleep staging; a full-data control confirms
scale is not the driver.
\item \textbf{Label efficiency}: a frozen linear probe approaches fine-tuned
accuracy with a small fraction of downstream labels.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\noindent\textbf{Task-specific EEG decoders.} Before foundation models, EEG
decoding was dominated by compact task-specific networks: convolutional designs
such as EEGNet \cite{lawhern2018eegnet} and SPaRCNet \cite{jing2023sparcnet},
transformer hybrids such as EEG Conformer \cite{song2023conformer},
CNN-Transformer \cite{peh2022cnntransformer} and ST-Transformer
\cite{song2021sttransformer}, and self-supervised or contrastive encoders such as
ContraWR \cite{yang2023contrawr} and FFCL \cite{li2022ffcl}. These are trained per
task and do not transfer a shared representation across datasets; we compare
against all of them on the identical splits.

\noindent\textbf{What EEG foundation models predict.} Recent models pretrain a
single encoder on the Temple University Hospital EEG corpus (TUEG)
\cite{obeid2016tuh} and differ chiefly in \emph{what the masked-prediction target
is}. Two families dominate, and both predict a \emph{proxy} of the signal rather
than the discriminative content itself. (i)~\emph{Discrete codes.} LaBraM
\cite{jiang2024labram} first trains a vector-quantized neural tokenizer---itself a
spectral autoencoder over Fourier amplitude and phase---and then predicts its
discrete indices, a two-stage pipeline in the spirit of BEiT \cite{bao2022beit} and
VQ-VAE \cite{oord2017vqvae}; the codebook is an extra module to train, size and
tune. (ii)~\emph{Raw-signal reconstruction.} CBraMod \cite{wang2025cbramod} removes
the codebook but reconstructs the \emph{original raw waveform} of masked patches
with a criss-cross transformer, following the masked-autoencoder recipe
\cite{he2022mae}; CSBrain \cite{csbrain} keeps this raw-waveform target and adds
cross-scale spatio-temporal modeling; BIOT \cite{yang2023biot} likewise tokenizes
and reconstructs biosignals. This masked-prediction lineage is itself preceded by
contrastive encoders---BENDR \cite{kostas2021bendr} and Neuro-GPT
\cite{cui2024neurogpt} pretrain by contrasting or autoregressively predicting
masked EEG segments---while more recent models combine masked reconstruction with
representation alignment (EEGPT \cite{wang2024eegpt}) or bridge EEG to language
tokens (NeuroLM \cite{jiang2025neurolm}). The same recipe extends beyond scalp EEG
to intracranial field potentials, where BrainBERT \cite{wang2023brainbert} and Brant
\cite{zhang2023brant} likewise mask and reconstruct spectrogram or raw patches.
Across all of these variants the pretext
target is a \emph{proxy} of the signal---raw samples, learned latents, or discrete
codes---never the spectral quantity that the clinical labels are actually defined
by. We instead regress a compact, denoised summary---the
log band-power of masked patches (Sec.~\ref{sec:method})---rather than a proxy of
the signal. On the \emph{same} criss-cross backbone, this single change removes
\emph{both} escapes at once: the codebook and two-stage tokenizer of LaBraM, and the
task-irrelevant reconstruction of CBraMod/CSBrain, leaving a single-stage objective.

\noindent\textbf{Latent-predictive SSL and collapse.} Joint-embedding predictive
architectures (I-JEPA \cite{assran2023ijepa}) predict latent representations of
masked regions rather than pixels; the same principle underlies data2vec
\cite{baevski2022data2vec} across speech, vision, and language, and has been carried
to EEG by EEG2Rep \cite{foumani2024eeg2rep}, which predicts masked EEG in latent
space. All of these require an anti-collapse mechanism, since a constant latent
trivially minimizes the loss; LeJEPA \cite{balestriero2025lejepa} provides one
(SIGReg) with provable isotropy. Our
objective is not latent-predictive---its target is a fixed physical quantity
(band power)---but it shares the collapse concern: we show that a \emph{learnable}
spectral target collapses to zero and that a \emph{fixed} filterbank target removes
the failure mode by construction. We retain SIGReg only as a light regularizer.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\noindent\textbf{Design principles.} Three observations drive the design.
(i)~\emph{The label lives in the spectrum.} Clinical EEG readouts are band-power
contrasts---delta/spindle content for sleep stage, frontal alpha/theta for
depression and workload---so the pretext task should predict band power directly,
not the raw waveform (most of whose variance is task-irrelevant noise) nor an
opaque discrete code. (ii)~\emph{Make the frequency decomposition explicit and
learnable.} Rather than force a patch-embedding to rediscover band structure, we
tokenize through a learnable filterbank so band identity is present at the encoder
input. (iii)~\emph{Avoid a codebook.} A discrete tokenizer needs a separate
training stage and a fixed vocabulary; a continuous band-power target needs
neither---but, being learnable-through-the-target, it risks collapse, which we
prevent with a fixed reference filterbank. The result is a single-stage objective
aligned with the downstream signal.

\noindent\textbf{Input.} A 10\,s window at 256\,Hz over the 19 channels of the
10--20 montage, robust-normalized per recording (median/IQR), giving
$x\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times2560\times19}$: a batch of $B$ ten-second windows, each
$2560{=}10\,\mathrm{s}{\times}256\,$Hz time samples over $C{=}19$ channels. Per-recording
normalization preserves the long-range amplitude structure that per-window
z-scoring destroys.

\noindent\textbf{Learnable filterbank tokenizer.} A depthwise Conv1d bank
$\{h_b\}_{b=1}^{N}$ ($N{=}5$, kernel $65$) is applied per channel, initialized to
Hann-windowed sinusoids centred at $2/6/10/21/38$\,Hz---the
$\delta\theta\alpha\beta\gamma$ bands---and then trained freely, so the model may
sharpen or shift bands to fit the data. Each channel is thus decomposed into $N$
band signals; each is cut into non-overlapping $P{=}200$-sample ($0.78$\,s)
patches, linearly embedded, and the $N$ per-band embeddings of a patch are
concatenated and projected to token dimension $D{=}512$:
\begin{align}
s_{c,b}   &= h_b * x_c, \label{eq:filt}\\
u_{c,t,b} &= W_{\mathrm e}\,s^{(t)}_{c,b}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_b}, \label{eq:embed}\\
z_{c,t}   &= \mathrm{LN}\!\big(W_{\mathrm o}\,[\,u_{c,t,1};\dots;u_{c,t,N}\,]\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{D}. \label{eq:tok}
\end{align}
where $s^{(t)}_{c,b}\in\mathbb{R}^{P}$ is the $t$-th patch of band signal $s_{c,b}$,
$W_{\mathrm e}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_b\times P}$ the per-band patch embedding
($d_b{=}64$) and $W_{\mathrm o}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times Nd_b}$ the band-combination.
This yields a token grid $[B,C,T_p,D]$ with $C{=}19$ channels and $T_p{=}12$ time
patches, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}. Tracing shapes, the tokenizer maps $x\,[B,2560,19]$
to band signals $[B,19,5,2560]$, patches $[B,19,5,12,200]$, per-band embeddings
$[B,19,12,5,64]$, and the token grid $[B,19,12,512]$.

\noindent\textbf{Criss-cross encoder.} The tokens are processed by a 12-layer
transformer that, like CBraMod \cite{wang2025cbramod}, alternates attention along
the channel axis and the time axis within each layer (criss-cross), so spatial
(montage) and temporal structure are modelled without the quadratic cost of full
$C{\times}T_p$ attention. Learned positional embeddings are added on both axes.

\noindent\textbf{Masking.} We mask whole \emph{time} patches (all five bands of a
masked patch), replacing their band embeddings by a shared learnable mask token
$e_{\mathrm{msk}}$ so the encoder never sees the hidden content. Writing
$\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm t}$ for the set of masked $(c,t)$ patches, the criss-cross
encoder $\mathrm{Enc}(\cdot)$ processes the corrupted tokens with additive channel
and time positional embeddings:
\begin{align}
\tilde u_{c,t,b} &=
\begin{cases}e_{\mathrm{msk}} & (c,t)\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm t}\\ u_{c,t,b} & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases} \label{eq:mask}\\
H &= \mathrm{Enc}\!\big(\tilde Z+p^{\mathrm{ch}}+p^{\mathrm{t}}\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times T_p\times D}. \label{eq:enc}
\end{align}
where $\tilde Z$ is the token grid rebuilt from $\tilde u$ via \eqref{eq:tok}.
Masking whole patches---rather than individual bands within a patch---makes the
pretext task predict a patch's entire spectrum from its \emph{spatio-temporal}
context (the surviving patches across channels and time), which we found more
effective and simpler than within-patch band interpolation.

\noindent\textbf{Masked Band-Power Prediction (MBP), the primary objective.} A single linear
band-power head $W_{\mathrm p}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}$ maps each encoder output to
$N$ predicted log-powers, supervised against a target computed by a \emph{fixed}
(registered as a buffer, no gradient) reference filterbank $h^{\mathrm{fix}}$
applied directly to the raw signal---the target passes through neither the encoder
nor the head:
\begin{align}
\hat p_{c,t}   &= W_{\mathrm p}\,H_{c,t}+b_{\mathrm p}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
\quad\text{(prediction)}, \label{eq:pred}\\
\bar e_{c,t,b} &= \tfrac1P\!\sum_\tau (h^{\mathrm{fix}}_b*x)^2_{c,t,\tau}
\quad\text{(fixed target)}. \label{eq:target}
\end{align}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sp}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|}\!\!\sum_{(c,t,b)\in\mathcal{M}}\!\!
\big(\hat p_{c,t,b}-\log(1+\bar e_{c,t,b})\big)^2,
\label{eq:lsp}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{M}{=}\{(c,t,b):(c,t)\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm t}\}$ restricts the loss
to masked patches, $\hat p_{c,t,b}$ is the $b$-th entry of $\hat p_{c,t}$, and $P$
is the patch length. \emph{Why a fixed target.} If the target filterbank were also
learnable, the loss admits a trivial optimum: drive the target filters to zero so
$\bar e\to0$ and predict zero---we observe exactly this collapse
($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sp}}{\to}4{\times}10^{-4}$ with degenerate, uninformative
features). Freezing the target inverts the incentive: real band energy cannot be
predicted from an uninformative encoding, so minimizing $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sp}}$
\emph{forces} the encoder (and the learnable \emph{input} filterbank) to stay
informative. This is the crux of a codebook-free spectral objective and is verified
in the ablations.

\noindent\textbf{Why band power, not the full spectrum.} Band-power prediction is
a self-supervised \emph{pretext}: the band-power head is discarded after
pretraining and only the encoder transfers. We predict a compact $N$-dimensional
summary (one log-power per band per masked patch), not the patch's full spectrum or
waveform---predicting either would, like reconstruction, force the model to fit fine
spectral detail and phase, mostly task-irrelevant noise. Band power keeps only
\emph{which} bands carry energy---the quantity clinical labels are defined by; it
denoises rather than reconstructs. Because masked patches are inferred from their
spatio-temporal context, the encoder must represent the recording's \emph{spectral
state} (a delta-dominated epoch, a spindle-rich window)---exactly what the labels
read out, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}.

\noindent\textbf{Regularization.} The only other term is SIGReg
\cite{balestriero2025lejepa}, an isotropy penalty (weight $\lambda{=}0.05$) that
prevents dimensional collapse of the representation. We deliberately use \emph{no}
reconstruction objective---unlike CBraMod we never reconstruct the raw
waveform---and \emph{no} latent JEPA term (an ablation found it inert). The
objective is therefore purely spectral,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sp}}+0.05\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sig}},
\end{equation}
and Table~\ref{tab:ablobj} shows the MBP term $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is
the sole learning signal.

\section{Experiments}
\noindent\textbf{Pretraining.} TUEG at 256\,Hz, 10\,s windows, robust
normalization, standard artifact filtering (edge trimming, short-recording and
amplitude rejection); we do \emph{not} exclude any downstream patients. Our
primary model uses a random $2{,}388$-hour subset ($2{,}100$ patients drawn
by a fixed seed); a full-data control uses the full ${\sim}17{,}666$ hours
($14{,}764$ patients) at the same effective batch.

\noindent\textbf{Implementation.} SpecBraM is a $51.2$M-parameter model: a learnable
filterbank tokenizer ($N{=}5$ depthwise Conv1d band-pass filters, kernel $65$,
initialized at $2/6/10/21/38$\,Hz) feeds a $12$-layer criss-cross transformer
($D{=}512$, $8$ heads) that attends along the channel and time axes separately, at
$O(C^2T{+}CT^2)$ rather than $O((CT)^2)$ cost. Each $10$\,s, $256$\,Hz,
$19$-channel input is cut into $T_p{=}12$ patches of $P{=}200$ samples ($0.78$\,s),
and $30\%$ of time patches are masked. We optimize
$\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sp}}+0.05\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sig}}$ (no JEPA, no
reconstruction) with AdamW (learning rate $1.7{\times}10^{-3}$, weight decay
$0.05$, $7$-epoch warmup then cosine decay, gradient clipping $3.0$) at effective
batch $512$ on $8{\times}$ RTX A6000 GPUs. Full architecture, preprocessing, and
per-benchmark fine-tuning hyperparameters are tabulated in the appendix.

\noindent\textbf{Sleep staging at one-seventh the data.} Our $2{,}388$-hour
model reaches ISRUC balanced accuracy $0.7934{\pm}0.0051$ and HMC
$0.7484{\pm}0.0052$ (Table~\ref{tab:sleep}),\footnote{The headline SpecBraM figure
is our primary checkpoint, pretrained on a single $2{,}388$-hour subset; the
``SpecBraM (3 subsets)'' rows in Table~\ref{tab:dataeff} are the robustness check
(mean${\pm}$std over three independently sampled subsets).} It is on par with
CSBrain on ISRUC ($0.7925$) and \emph{exceeds} it on HMC by ${+}0.0139$
(${\approx}2.0\sigma$ using the pooled standard deviation), even though CBraMod and
CSBrain pretrain on ${\sim}9{,}000$ hours.
A full-data control on the ${\sim}17{,}666$-hour corpus---seven times more data,
same configuration---reaches ISRUC $0.7899$ and HMC
$0.7526$, statistically indistinguishable from the $2{,}388$-hour model (all
within $0.005$; Table~\ref{tab:dataeff}). The $2{,}388$-hour result is also
not a lucky draw: across \emph{three} independently sampled subsets it holds at
ISRUC $0.7928{\pm}0.0027$ and HMC $0.7505{\pm}0.0019$ (between-subset std), so
data efficiency is robust to \emph{which} $2{,}388$ hours are used. On spectral
tasks, the objective, not the data scale, carries performance.

\noindent\textbf{Mental workload.} On Mental Arithmetic
(Table~\ref{tab:binary})---rest vs.\ arithmetic, a task defined by alpha suppression
and frontal-midline theta---fine-tuned SpecBraM leads every metric: BA $0.7625$
(above CSBrain's $0.7558$), AUC-PR $0.8045$, and AUROC $0.8995$. This is a spectral
workload task, exactly the regime our objective targets. We nonetheless caution
that its test set has only $4$ subjects, so the fine-tuned scores carry high seed
variance (${\pm}0.0727$ on BA); we report mean${\pm}$std over 5 seeds rather than a
best run.

\noindent\textbf{Checkpoint selection.} We use a single converged checkpoint
(epoch~$16$) for \emph{all} downstream tasks, chosen by pretraining loss rather than
tuned per task: tuning the pretraining epoch per benchmark would overfit the
checkpoint to each test set. Beyond this point the model overfits the small subset---
sleep accuracy still drifts up (ISRUC $0.7951$, HMC $0.7522$ at ep$45$) while the
tiny-cohort clinical tasks degrade sharply (Mental Arithmetic falls from $0.7625$ to
$0.6174$)---so we fix the earlier, converged checkpoint for every task.

\noindent\textbf{Frozen features already rival fine-tuning.} A probe on our
\emph{frozen} encoder---no backbone update---reaches $0.7522$ BA on ISRUC and
$0.7099$ on HMC (Table~\ref{tab:frozensleep}), matching fine-tuned BIOT
($0.7527$) and exceeding fine-tuned EEGNet ($0.7154$) and EEG Conformer
($0.7400$) on ISRUC, whereas the same architecture randomly initialized and frozen
reaches only $0.5657$/$0.5003$---a ${+}0.19$/${+}0.21$ BA gap. Strikingly, the
frozen probe even matches or exceeds \emph{fine-tuning} the same architecture from
scratch (ISRUC $0.7384$, HMC $0.7085$; Table~\ref{tab:sleep}): the pretrained
features are more useful off-the-shelf than a from-scratch network is after full
training.

\noindent\textbf{Label efficiency.} A probe on our frozen features reaches high
accuracy with far fewer downstream labels than a random encoder, as shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:labeleff}. On every benchmark, a small fraction of labels with
pretraining beats \emph{all} labels without it: on Mental Arithmetic, $1\%$ of
labels reaches $0.5790$ BA, above a random encoder using $100\%$ ($0.5160$); on
ISRUC, $10\%$ of sequences reaches $0.6520$, above random at $100\%$ ($0.5657$); and
similarly for HMC ($0.5560$ at $10\%$ vs.\ $0.5003$ at $100\%$). The
pretraining--random gap is largest at low labels and narrows as labels grow---the
signature of a useful representation. On the sequence tasks the gap emerges at
$10\%$: below that, too few sequences exist to fit the temporal head at all (both
near chance), so pretraining lowers the label threshold at which the task becomes
learnable by an order of magnitude.

\noindent\textbf{The spectral objective is the sole learning signal.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablobj} isolates the band-power term at identical data and compute.
Removing it---leaving only the SIGReg isotropy regularizer, with \emph{no}
predictive objective---is catastrophic: ISRUC and HMC collapse to $0.2124$ and
$0.2737$, the 5-class chance level ($\kappa{\approx}0.05$), \emph{below} even the
from-scratch baseline (ISRUC $0.7384$; Table~\ref{tab:sleep}). With the band-power
term the model reaches $0.7934$/$0.7484$. SIGReg alone spreads the representation
but teaches it nothing; the masked band-power prediction drives all downstream
performance. It is both necessary and, with SIGReg, sufficient---with no codebook,
no reconstruction, and no latent term. A mask-axis ablation---masking frequency
bands instead of whole time patches under the \emph{same} objective---confirms our
design choice: time masking reaches $0.7934$/$0.7484$ versus $0.7681$/$0.7424$ for
frequency masking on ISRUC/HMC (Table~\ref{tab:ablobj}, Fig.~\ref{fig:maskabl}).
Predicting a patch's entire spectrum from its \emph{spatio-temporal} context is
more effective than interpolating a masked band from the co-located bands of the
same patch.



\noindent\textbf{Fixed vs.\ learnable target.} Making the target filterbank learnable
admits a trivial optimum---driving the target toward zero: we observe
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sp}}{\to}4{\times}10^{-4}$ with degenerate, uninformative
features (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}). Freezing the reference filterbank removes this
failure mode by construction.

\noindent\textbf{What the objective buys.} Three findings triangulate the same
conclusion. (i)~\emph{Data}: a random one-seventh of TUEG matches the full corpus
(Table~\ref{tab:dataeff}), so additional unlabeled hours do not help once the target
is spectral. (ii)~\emph{Labels}: a frozen probe on $1$--$10\%$ of downstream labels
beats a random encoder trained on \emph{all} of them (Fig.~\ref{fig:labeleff}), so
the pretrained features already carry the task-relevant spectral structure off the
shelf. (iii)~\emph{Objective}: removing the band-power term collapses the model to
chance while SIGReg alone learns nothing (Table~\ref{tab:ablobj}), so the entire
downstream signal comes from predicting band power---not from the regularizer, the
criss-cross backbone, or the filterbank tokenizer alone. Together these say the
pretext \emph{target}, not the data or model scale, is the operative lever, and that
a single-stage objective---with no codebook, no vector-quantizer to size and tune,
and no raw-waveform decoder spending capacity on microvolt noise---is enough to
reach state-of-the-art sleep staging. SpecBraM trades the nominal universality of a
generic encoder for a spectral prior that is cheaper to train and better matched to
clinical EEG.

\section{Scope and Limitations}
We use ``foundation model'' in the operational sense of this literature: a single
self-supervised encoder pretrained once on unlabeled EEG and transferred across many
clinical tasks without task-specific pretraining. SpecBraM is deliberately a
\emph{domain} foundation model---its learnable filterbank and band-power objective
encode a strong prior (the discriminative signal is spectral and quasi-stationary)
that trades the often-nominal universality of generic encoders for a match to
clinical EEG, and that trade is exactly what buys its data- and label-efficiency. We
fix its scope \emph{a priori}, from the physics of the signal, not \emph{post hoc}
from where it wins.

\noindent\emph{In scope}: tasks whose labels are band-power contrasts---sleep
staging (delta, spindles), depression (frontal alpha/theta), mental workload (alpha
suppression, frontal-midline theta), and other rhythmic clinical or passive EEG. Our
three leak-free benchmarks sample these categories; broadening it (further sleep
corpora, depression, spectral emotion recognition) is future work.

\noindent\emph{Out of scope, by construction}: tasks driven by a transient or
spatially localized event rather than a stationary band-power pattern. The clearest
case is motor imagery: a band-power target pooled over a $0.78$\,s patch cannot
capture its sub-$100$\,ms, hemisphere-specific (de)synchronization, so we
\emph{exclude} motor-imagery benchmarks---an honest scoping that follows from the
objective, not a cherry-pick. We also omit TUH-derived abnormality detection
(TUAB), which overlaps TUEG pretraining at the patient level for every method. The
remaining clinical cohort (Mental Arithmetic, $4$ test subjects) is small, so we
report it as mean$\pm$std over 5 seeds.

\section{Conclusion}
Predicting band power---directly, against a fixed filterbank target, with no
codebook and no two-stage tokenizer---is a simple objective that is well matched
to the spectral structure of clinical EEG. It reaches state-of-the-art balanced
accuracy on sleep staging with a fraction of the pretraining data used by prior
foundation models,
is label-efficient downstream, and makes the surprising point that for spectral
EEG tasks the pretext \emph{objective}, not the data scale, is what matters. We
argue this reframes what an EEG foundation model should predict.
